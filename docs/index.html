    How it decides

    1. Cutoff — the highest frequency still carrying real energy, taken from a 95th-percentile long-term spectrum. A percentile rather than a mean, because quiet passages drag a mean into the noise floor.
    2. Steepness — how much further the level falls within 1 kHz above that cutoff. An encoder's brick wall drops 20 dB or more; natural rolloff does not.
    3. Intensity stereo — lossy codecs collapse L/R into mono-plus-weight at high frequencies, so the side signal (L−R)/2 vanishes above some frequency. Independent of the lowpass.

    Read a clean verdict as “no lowpass evidence”, not as proof of provenance. Encoders that apply no hard lowpass — ffmpeg's native AAC at higher bitrates, Opus — pass this test. And because browsers resample on decode, anything above 21.6 kHz is treated as full-band.
